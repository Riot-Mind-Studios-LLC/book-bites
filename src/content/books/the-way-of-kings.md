## The Way of Kings Review

### A Prologue That Brought Snacks

The book opens with a prologue, followed by a second prologue, followed by a prologue that insists it is actually the first chapter. All three are wearing the same coat. A narrator steps forward to sort things out but is promptly elected treasurer of the table of contents.

The pacing is relaxed. Several paragraphs take a nap.

I found this deeply reassuring.

---

### Plot, According To Witnesses

The story follows a locksmith attempting to open a door that only exists on weekends. Progress is slowed by a number of obstacles:

- a staircase that charges admission in compliments
- a river that has filed for early retirement
- a choir of teapots rehearsing a very long hum
- a hallway that keeps winning arguments it never started

Around chapter eleven a weathervane becomes a minor celebrity. It handles the fame poorly.

---

### People And Objects Of Note

The cast is large, earnest, and frequently lost.

- **Ottoline Brasswick** – The locksmith. Owns four hundred keys and trusts none of them.
- **Sergeant Pellum** – Guards a gate that nobody has ever tried to enter.
- **The Weathervane** – Points north, mostly out of spite.
- **A Goat Named Councilor Higgs** – Votes on every issue, usually by eating the ballot.

Character growth happens gradually, like moss on a confused rock.

---

### Themes That Wandered In

The novel brushes against several large ideas:

- the moral responsibilities of doors
- whether rivers deserve pensions
- the quiet heroism of well-organized drawers
- the cultural significance of slightly burnt toast

A footnote suggests the whole book is a metaphor for laundry. The laundry declines to comment.

---

### Favourite Passage

> The door considered the key for a long moment, sighed in the manner of a tired cathedral, and asked whether anyone had tried knocking.

The passage is four sentences long and somehow smells faintly of rain.

---

### Final Thoughts

The ending arrives on schedule, which surprised everyone involved. Most plot threads are tied into a tidy bow, though one escapes and is last seen heading toward the sequel.

Recommended for readers who enjoy:

- prologues that multiply when unattended
- architecture with strong opinions
- livestock in positions of civic authority
- a climactic standoff between a key and its own reflection

Overall rating: **9 out of 10 cautiously optimistic doorknobs**.

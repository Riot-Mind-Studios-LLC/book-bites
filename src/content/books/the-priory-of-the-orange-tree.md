## The Priory of the Orange Tree Review

### A Very Long Book That Knows It

The novel opens by apologizing for its size and offering the reader a small cushion. The first chapter then clears its throat for several pages before admitting it has not yet decided where to begin. A map is provided. The map is also lost.

The tone is grand and ceremonial. Even the semicolons appear to be wearing robes.

I read it in sections, like a cathedral.

---

### Plot, With Footnotes

The story follows an orchard keeper attempting to teach an orange tree to grow apples. The tree has strong objections, and the project faces several difficulties:

- a garden wall that demands a formal title before it will hold anything up
- a beehive governed by a constitutional monarchy
- a fountain that only flows during polite conversation
- a wheelbarrow with a long and dramatic past

Midway through, the orange tree produces a single lemon out of protest. It is treated as a diplomatic incident.

---

### Members Of The Orchard

The characters are dignified, devoted, and slightly sticky.

- **Wilhelmina Thorne** – The orchard keeper. Believes every tree can change if it feels supported.
- **The Orange Tree** – Has no interest in apples. Never has. Never will.
- **Brother Quillon** – Keeps the orchard records in a ledger that grows fruit when ignored.
- **A Dragonfly Named Her Excellency** – Oversees all ceremonies and judges them harshly.

Characters grow slowly and in season, with occasional pruning.

---

### Themes, Lightly Peeled

The book seems to ponder several weighty ideas:

- the right of a tree to remain a tree
- the politics of beehives
- whether a lemon can be a form of protest
- the heavy burden of being a wheelbarrow

An inscription on the garden gate claims the whole tale is "a parable about patience and citrus." The gate refuses to elaborate.

---

### Favourite Passage

> The orange tree rustled its leaves in a long, regal sigh and informed the orchard keeper that it had considered her proposal, and that the answer was still oranges.

The passage is four lines long and smells faintly of marmalade.

---

### Final Thoughts

By the end, the orchard keeper accepts the tree as it is, and the tree, in return, produces one very small and very reluctant apple. Celebrations last three chapters. The lemon is placed in a museum.

Recommended for readers who enjoy:

- books that come with their own cushion
- trees with firm personal boundaries
- insects in high public office
- a tense negotiation between a garden wall and a ladder

Overall rating: **8 out of 10 regally stubborn orange trees**.

## Piranesi Review

### A House That Kept Adding Rooms

The novel opens in a hallway that leads to another hallway, which leads to a hallway that seems surprised to see anyone. The narrator keeps a careful journal of each room. The rooms, in turn, keep a careful journal of the narrator.

The prose is calm and orderly. A statue occasionally coughs.

I felt as though I should have taken my shoes off.

---

### Plot, Room By Room

The story follows an archivist attempting to count every room in a house that adds a new one whenever it is counted. The effort is complicated by:

- a tide that visits the lower floors on Thursdays
- a library where the shelves rearrange themselves by mood
- a staircase that leads up, then reconsiders
- a statue holding a sign that simply reads "Probably"

In the second act the archivist discovers a room containing only a single, very well-behaved sock. Its significance is debated at length.

---

### Residents Of The House

The characters are few, gentle, and deeply committed to their routines.

- **Ambrose Tull** – The archivist. Has numbered 7,412 rooms and is not discouraged.
- **The Other Visitor** – Arrives every Tuesday with an umbrella and a vague air of disappointment.
- **The Statue With The Sign** – Offers guidance that is technically accurate.
- **A Seagull Named Footnote** – Delivers messages, though rarely the correct ones.

Character development is slow and quiet, like a tide coming in under a door.

---

### Themes Found In The Hallways

The book seems to reflect on several patient ideas:

- the comfort of counting things that cannot be counted
- whether a house can be lonely
- the hospitality of tides
- the dignity of a single, unpaired sock

A note found inside a vase suggests the house is "listening very carefully." The vase has since been moved.

---

### Favourite Passage

> The tide came up the stairs, wiped its feet politely on the landing, and asked if the archivist had found the room with the good chairs yet.

The sentence feels like a warm room on a cold day.

---

### Final Thoughts

The ending opens a door the reader did not know was there. The count of rooms remains unfinished, which feels exactly right. The sock is placed in a position of honor.

Recommended for readers who appreciate:

- architecture that grows when no one is looking
- tides with excellent manners
- statues that speak in careful half-answers
- a quiet, moving reunion between a sock and its long-lost drawer

Overall rating: **10 out of 10 politely endless hallways**.

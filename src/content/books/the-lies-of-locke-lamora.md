### A Story That Arrived In Disguise

The novel begins by introducing itself as a cookbook. It maintains this claim for nearly two pages before a recipe for soup confesses to being a heist. From here the book commits fully to misdirection, including a chapter that pretends to be the index.

The narrator winks at the reader frequently. It is unclear why.

I checked my wallet twice.

---

### Plot, Under Questionable Oath

The story centers on a clockmaker attempting to steal ten minutes from the town square. The scheme encounters several complications:

- a sundial that refuses to cooperate after dark
- a bell tower with a gambling problem
- a market stall selling slightly used afternoons
- a canal that only flows in the direction of gossip

At one point the stolen minutes escape and hide inside a waltz. Recovery efforts are elegant but unsuccessful.

---

### The Usual Suspects

The characters are charming, unreliable, and occasionally made of brass.

- **Florian Vetch** – The clockmaker. Insists punctuality is a form of theft.
- **Madame Corvelle** – Owns the bell tower. Owes it a considerable amount of money.
- **Officer Dimm** – Investigates missing time but is always slightly late.
- **A Parrot Named Ledger** – Keeps the accounts and repeats them at inconvenient moments.

Every character is lying about something, including the furniture.

---

### Ideas Hidden In The Lining

The book quietly explores several themes:

- the property rights of unused minutes
- whether clocks can be held legally responsible
- the ethics of borrowing a Tuesday without asking
- the emotional burden of always being on time

A marginal note claims that honesty is overrated. The note was later found to be forged.

---

### Favourite Passage

> The clock struck thirteen, looked around nervously, and pretended it had meant to do that all along.

The moment is brief but leaves the reader checking the time.

---

### Final Thoughts

The conclusion reveals that the true heist was the friends made along the way, which is then revealed to also be a lie. Several readers report finding extra minutes in their pockets afterward.

Recommended for readers who appreciate:

- narrators who cannot be trusted with spoons
- timekeeping as a criminal enterprise
- birds with accounting experience
- a final twist involving an extremely smug pocket watch

Overall rating: **8 out of 10 suspiciously punctual pocket watches**.

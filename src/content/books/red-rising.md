### A Book That Was Painted Before It Was Written

The novel begins by explaining that everything in the story is color-coded. Unfortunately the color key has been misplaced. Readers are encouraged to guess. A beige paragraph attempts to help but is quickly overruled by a louder shade of orange.

The tone is intense. Several commas appear to be clenching their jaws.

I read most of it standing up.

---

### Plot, Broadly Shaded

The story concerns a painter attempting to repaint the sky a more reasonable color. This project meets considerable resistance:

- a sunset that files a formal complaint
- a paint shop that only sells colors that have not been invented
- a ladder with a fear of heights
- a crowd of clouds staging a sit-in

By chapter ten the sky has been painted plaid. Nobody is happy, but everybody is impressed.

---

### Characters In Various Shades

The cast is bold, dramatic, and frequently covered in paint.

- **Cassius Vermillion** – The painter. Believes the sky has been coasting for too long.
- **Lady Ochre** – Owns the paint shop. Refuses to sell anything in pastel.
- **The Ladder** – Determined but trembling. Requires constant encouragement.
- **A Very Large Moth Named Gerald** – Drawn to the project for reasons he will not discuss.

Characters change color depending on how they feel, which makes the dialogue tags unnecessary.

---

### Themes, Broad Brushstrokes

The book explores several vivid ideas:

- the political power of a well-chosen shade
- whether the sky has a right to stay blue
- ladders and the courage required to be climbed
- the long-term consequences of plaid

A note in the appendix warns that the entire story may be "a matter of taste." The appendix is printed in teal.

---

### Favourite Passage

> The sunset read the complaint aloud, cleared its throat in a deep shade of crimson, and demanded to speak to whoever was in charge of evenings.

The passage is short, loud, and leaves a faint stain on the page.

---

### Final Thoughts

The ending is dramatic, with the sky partially restored and partially redecorated. The plaid remains in one corner as a reminder. Gerald the moth is last seen circling the moon with renewed purpose.

Recommended for readers who enjoy:

- color as a form of government
- ladders with emotional journeys
- weather that knows how to organize
- a final confrontation between a paintbrush and the horizon

Overall rating: **7 out of 10 slightly smudged rainbows**.
